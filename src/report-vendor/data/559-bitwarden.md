## STAR stories

#### The SOC 2 pentest that needed an engineer, not a checkbox

> Finturf was mid-audit when it became clear that **half the required controls had no technical owner** — they existed on paper only. I took point on the pentest track: coordinated with the external auditors, mapped every open item to an owner, built the remediation backlog, and closed **80% of required controls** before the audit window closed. The remaining 20% were deferred with documented risk acceptance. Bitwarden's buyers are security teams — they'll ask about compliance posture in the first call. This story is the answer.

_Reflection: I'd scope the pentest backlog earlier and build the controls tracker as a living doc from day one, not a sprint before the deadline._

#### The partner that couldn't integrate for three months

> One of Finturf's enterprise partners spent **three months** failing at the OAuth handoff in our lending integration. I took it end-to-end: built a private sandbox environment the same day they escalated, wrote the integration guide from scratch, ran three live sessions with their engineering lead, and got them into production. That pattern — **own it until it works** — became the standard, and it's why partner onboarding dropped by **40%** across all new accounts.

_Reflection: I built the sandbox in a day; I should have offered it on the first call, not the third._

#### The AI chat that replaced a support tier

> Finturf's support team was fielding **the same 50 questions** in different forms from every new partner. I scoped, built, and deployed a RAG-based support chat — Pinecone for retrieval, GPT-4 for generation, a Python microservice for the API layer — in **six weeks**. It cut support tickets by **25%**. At Bitwarden, prospects ask the same LDAP, SSO, and Docker questions repeatedly; the answer is tooling, not a FAQ page — and building that tooling is the SE job I'd want.

_Reflection: The embedding refresh was manual for the first two months; I'd automate the pipeline trigger from day one._
